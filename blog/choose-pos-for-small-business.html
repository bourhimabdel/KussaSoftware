POS Basics

How to Choose POS Software for a Small Business

KUSOX Editorial Team  ·  7 min read

Choosing POS software is easier when you begin with your daily tasks rather than a long list of features. A small shop may need fast checkout and reliable stock counts; another may need multiple cashiers, detailed reporting, or several devices.

Write down your must-have tasks

List the jobs your current process handles: adding products, recording sales, printing receipts, tracking stock, reviewing past transactions, and closing the day. Separate essential tasks from features that would simply be nice to have.

Check product and inventory tools

  • Can you add, edit, deactivate, and search products easily?
  • Does the software support barcodes if you need them?
  • Can it record stock receipts, sales, returns, and adjustments?
  • Are product variants, units, or multiple locations supported where relevant?

Review reports and user access

Check whether reports answer the questions you ask regularly, such as sales by date, product, or cashier. If several employees use the system, review roles and permissions so staff have appropriate access to business data and settings.

Test the hardware you already own

Confirm operating-system support and compatibility with your receipt printer, scanner, cash drawer, tablet, or other equipment. Test the full workflow—not just whether the device connects. Printer languages, connection types, and operating-system support can differ.

Understand pricing and ownership of data

Compare subscription fees, payment processing, add-ons, support, hardware, taxes, and renewal terms. Find out whether you can export your product and sales records, how long data is retained, and what happens when you cancel.

Evaluate usability and support

Ask a staff member to complete typical tasks without guidance. Check documentation, support response options, update policy, and how the provider communicates outages or maintenance.

Run a realistic trial

Use representative products and test common situations such as a refund, a price change, a stock correction, and an end-of-day report. Keep notes on errors and unanswered questions, then compare candidates against the same checklist.

Final thoughts

The right POS should make important work more reliable without adding unnecessary complexity. Use a repeatable evaluation, confirm compatibility and plan limits, and choose the system that best fits your actual workflow.
