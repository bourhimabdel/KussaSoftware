How to Use a Barcode Scanner with POS Software

KUSOX Editorial Team  ·  6 min read

Barcode scanning can reduce manual typing at checkout and help staff select the correct product. The best results come from matching the scanner, barcode labels, and POS product list before using the setup during a busy shift.

How barcode scanning works

Most retail barcode scanners read the pattern printed on a label and send the decoded characters to a connected device. Many USB scanners behave like a keyboard: when a barcode is scanned, its number is entered into the focused field. Your POS software then looks for a product with that barcode.

Prepare your product records

  • Give each product a unique barcode value in your POS product list.
  • Check that the barcode on the physical item matches the value saved in the system.
  • For products sold in different sizes or variants, confirm each scannable variant maps to the correct item.
  • Print labels with enough contrast and avoid placing them over folds, seams, or reflective surfaces.

Connect and test the scanner

Connect a USB scanner to the computer or compatible device and follow the manufacturer's instructions. Some wireless models use a USB receiver or Bluetooth pairing. Open a safe test field first and scan a few labels to confirm the expected characters appear. Then test inside your POS using a non-production or clearly identified test item where possible.

Choose a scanner type

USB wired scanners are often straightforward for fixed checkout counters. Wireless scanners can help when staff need to scan items away from the counter, but require charging or battery management. Rugged handheld devices may suit warehouses or environments where equipment receives heavy use.

Troubleshoot common problems

  • Nothing appears: check the cable, receiver, pairing, battery, and whether the correct input field is active.
  • Wrong product opens: compare the scanned number with the barcode stored in the product record.
  • Only some labels fail: inspect print quality, damage, glare, and barcode size.
  • Extra characters appear: check scanner suffix or prefix settings and the keyboard layout.
  • Scanning works in a text editor but not in POS: verify field focus and the POS barcode workflow.

Set up a reliable daily routine

Keep the scanner lens clean, check wireless battery levels, maintain readable labels, and train staff on what to do when a barcode is missing. A manual product search should remain available as a fallback.

Final thoughts

A scanner is only one part of the checkout process. Accurate product records, suitable labels, and a quick test of the complete POS workflow are just as important as the hardware.
